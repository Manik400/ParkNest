# 2026-08-24 (later) — the slot with somebody else's car in it

Closed the open product question from Phase 1, or rather the half of it that was never a product
question.

## What was open

> Nothing prevents a renter overstaying into another renter's booked slot. The credit system bills
> them, but the second renter still has nowhere to park.

It had sat as an open question because the obvious next step — compensating the renter who was
turned away — needs a decision nobody has taken: refund and re-house, or let them take the loss and
pay them something. Each answer is a different ledger shape.

Waiting on that had a cost that was easy to miss. Until the decision is taken, the system does not
merely fail to compensate the second renter — it does not **notice** them. Nobody is told, nothing
is recorded, and the booking sits in `Held` looking perfectly ordinary right up until they arrive.
That part is wrong under every candidate policy, so it shipped without one.

## What it does now

The overstay meter looks, on every sweep, for a booking on the same space starting within
`Platform:BlockedSlotLookaheadMinutes`. When it finds one it writes `BlockedByBookingId` and
`BlockedAt` on that booking, publishes `NextSlotBlocked`, and three people hear about it:

- the renter on their way — *the space may still be occupied, cancelling costs you nothing*;
- the renter still parked — *someone is waiting, please move now*;
- the host — *your space is double-occupied*, because they are the only one who can go and look.

And the flag makes that booking free to cancel however late it is.

## Four decisions worth keeping

**Detection runs outside the overstay grace period.** The meter's sweep used to select sessions
past `ExpectedEndTime - OverstayGraceMinutes`; it now selects everything past `ExpectedEndTime` and
applies grace only to the billing. Grace is a courtesy about *when a charge lands* — it has nothing
to say about whose slot is occupied, and a renter fifteen minutes short of being billed is
fifteen minutes into somebody else's booking. There is a test that pins exactly this: one minute
over, nothing charged, block flagged.

**The free cancellation is the part that needed no policy decision.** A late-cancellation fee
compensates a host for notice too short to re-let the slot. There is no slot to re-let, and the
renter is not changing their mind — they are being turned away. Charging there would bill somebody
for our failure to deliver, which is the single thing that would make the whole cancellation policy
read as a trap. There is a control test asserting the same cancellation costs ₹15 without the flag.

**The flag is set once and never cleared.** Two reasons, and the second is the real one. Re-checking
each sweep would send the same warning every minute. But clearing it when the car finally moves
would withdraw a free cancellation from somebody who has already been told they have one and has
probably already turned around — worse than never offering it. So `WasBlocked` is a record of a
promise, not a live state, and there is a test that ends the over-running session and asserts the
cancellation is still free.

**`slotBlocked` is separate from `isFree` in the API.** Both clients need to say *why*. "Free
because you are early" and "free because we could not give you the space" are different sentences,
and a renter who reads the first when the second is true will conclude they got lucky rather than
that something went wrong.

## Everywhere it surfaces

`NextSlotBlocked` is handled in the same three places every other event is — durable notification,
SignalR push, Prometheus counter — so nothing about it is special-cased.

- **Counter:** `parknest_blocked_slots_total`, on the dashboard's over-runs panel. Worth watching
  next to overstays rather than instead of them: an overstay costs the renter money and the system
  handles it, this one costs a second renter a journey and the platform a refund it cannot recover.
- **Alert:** `BlockedSlotRateElevated`, warning, above 1% of bookings over six hours. The runbook
  says to check whether it is one host before touching platform-wide configuration, and that
  raising the lookahead fixes nothing — it only tells people earlier.
- **Console:** a banner on the booking linking to the session that over-ran, a red line in the
  bookings list, and the cancellation copy.
- **App:** the same three, plus the cancel dialog.

While in `ops/on-call.md` I fixed two runbook headings that named alerts (`HighShortfallRate`,
`HighDisputeRate`) which do not exist under those names in `alerts.yml` — they are
`ShortfallRateHigh` and `DisputeRateElevated`. A runbook section nobody can find from the alert
that fired is a runbook section that does not exist.

## Verification

300 unit tests (up from 288, twelve of them new), 36 Flutter tests (up from 33), `flutter analyze`
clean, the Angular console builds.

Exercised end to end against the live database rather than only in tests. Backdating is refused at
the API, so this ran on a real clock against a throwaway instance configured with one-minute
billing increments and no grace: two renters, one space, back to back, credits bought through the
sandbox gateway's own signed webhook. Before the sweep, cancelling the second booking cost ₹15 of a
₹30 hold. After it: `blockedByBookingId` set, `slotBlocked` true, fee ₹0, refund ₹30, the counter
at 1, and all three notifications stored with the right wording against the right booking ids.

## Still open, and still not code

Unchanged from this morning: Razorpay against the live API and the payout leg with it, a Firebase
project, and names in the on-call rota.

Newly open, and genuinely a product question rather than a placeholder for one: whether a renter
whose slot was blocked gets anything beyond their hold back, and who funds it — the over-running
renter's overstay charges, the host, or the platform.
